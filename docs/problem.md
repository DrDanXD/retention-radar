# The problem

## Who this is for

Membership longevity and preventive health clinics. Members pay a yearly fee for testing, a care team and ongoing coaching. Published pricing for well known clinics runs from about $8,000 to over $85,000 a year [1].

## What goes wrong

The product is a relationship that runs for a year, but the clinic only sees the member a few times inside it: an onboarding workup, periodic lab draws, scheduled reviews. Between those moments the member is on their own with a wearable, a habit plan and a message thread.

That is where members drift. Nobody quits on a Tuesday. The watch goes into a drawer. A couple of coach messages go unread. The morning walk stops. The next lab draw gets skipped. By the time the renewal invoice lands, the decision was made weeks ago.

Each lost member costs the clinic their full annual fee, plus the referrals they would have sent.

## Why current tools miss it

Clinics typically watch two things:

* **Portal logins.** Members keep logging in to read results even while they are deciding to leave. Logins measure curiosity, not commitment.
* **NPS surveys.** Sent a few times a year, answered by a minority, and a lagging read of sentiment. Research on NPS as a growth predictor finds it is not reliably better than ordinary satisfaction measures [2].

Neither looks at what members actually do day to day, and that is where the drift shows first.

Wearable abandonment is common: one widely cited study found a third of US wearable buyers stopped using the device within six months [3]. For a clinic whose program runs through that device, a quiet tracker is one of the clearest early signs.

## What Retention Radar does

1. Reads the signals the clinic already has: wearable syncs, coach message replies, habit logs, lab bookings, coach calls.
2. Scores each member with five plain rules, so every flag comes with the reasons behind it.
3. Puts a dollar figure on renewals at risk in the next 60 days.
4. Drafts a check-in in the coach's voice. The coach reviews and sends it. Nothing goes to a member automatically.
